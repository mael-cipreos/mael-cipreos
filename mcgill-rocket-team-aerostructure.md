# Leading the Aerostructure Subteam of a Two-Stage Supersonic Rocket

**McGill Rocket Team, Aerostructure Subteam Lead, Montreal, Canada**

## Context

The McGill Rocket Team designs and builds a two-stage supersonic rocket engineered to reach 25,000 m of altitude and a maximum velocity of 760 m/s. The aerostructure is everything that has to survive that: the airframe, the structural components, and the mechanism that separates the stages cleanly mid-flight. I lead the subteam responsible for all of it.

## What I do

I manage six aerostructure projects in parallel, each with a cross-functional team of 11 members, on a subteam budget of 5,000 CAD. My job is the full lifecycle of each component: concept, CAD, structural analysis, iteration, and the manufacturing drawings that the machine shop actually builds from.

The hardest piece is the stage separation mechanism. It has to hold two rocket stages together through launch and Max-Q, then let go exactly once, exactly on time. There is no partial credit.

## Tools

Siemens NX for CAD and finite element analysis (FEA), rigorous technical drawings for manufacturing

## Results

- Six aerostructure projects delivered through full design cycles, from model to manufacturing drawings.
- Over two years of design iterations with structural integrity verified by FEA before anything is built.
- A working division of labor across 11-person teams that keeps six projects moving at once.

## What I took away

Leading a technical subteam taught me that engineering and leadership are two halves of the same job. A design is only done when the analysis passes, the drawing is unambiguous, and the person manufacturing it understands exactly what you meant. That only happens when the people doing the work feel heard and respected, so I put as much care into how I work with my team as into the analysis itself.
